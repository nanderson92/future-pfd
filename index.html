FSA-004 · Reader protocol

How to read each technology card.

  1. Identify the sci-fi capability people imagine.
  2. Trace the material and energy inputs.
  3. Find the unit operations that turn inputs into outputs.
  4. Look for bottlenecks: energy, separations, yield, reliability, infrastructure.
  5. Ask what has to become repeatable before deployment.

FSA-007 · Unit operation library

The repeated failure modes behind future systems.

Different futures often fail at the same process steps: separating dilute molecules, moving heat, preventing fouling, depositing uniform films, purifying trace contaminants, controlling interfaces, and proving reliability over thousands of cycles.

Matter movement

Separations, filtration, membranes, adsorption.

Energy movement

Heat exchange, thermal management, regeneration.

Surface / interface control

Coatings, deposition, fouling, adhesion.

Manufacturing proof

QA gates, reliability cycling, impurity control, process windows.

FSA-008 · Bottlenecks and readiness

Most future systems are blocked by MRL, IRL, and process economics.

TRL

Does it work?

Technology readiness asks whether the core mechanism or device has been demonstrated.

MRL

Can we make it repeatedly?

Manufacturing readiness asks whether the process can meet yield, quality, cost, and throughput targets.

IRL

Can it integrate?

Infrastructure readiness asks whether utilities, logistics, standards, maintenance, and users can support it.

$

Can it survive outside a grant-funded demo?

Economics asks whether the full system can compete when capital, energy, labor, reliability, and utilization are counted.

FSA-012 · Evidence layer

Evidence appendix for technical reviewers.

For technical reviewers: inspect the evidence bank. Section claims, cards, compact rows, and detail modals carry source chips, source-support notes, and limitation language. Normal readers can stop before this appendix; the source layer exists to make the project auditable.

Open the searchable evidence bank and caution map
Use these citations cautiously.

Broad sources support the engineering lens; direct card sources support specific mechanisms or bottlenecks. Cost, timeline, deployment, and performance claims still need primary or current project-specific evidence. Speculative cards are intentionally marked as analogue-sourced rather than proven.

Evidence QA

Cards that need cautious wording.

These entries have useful sources, but the source support is mainly roadmap-stage, early-stage, or analogue-based. Do not present them as deployed systems.

FSA-011 · Author note

Built as a process-engineering deliverable, not a sci-fi directory.

I built Future Systems Atlas as a Chemical & Biomolecular Engineering student interested in the gap between frontier invention and real-world deployment. The project reflects the way I want to think as a process engineer: not just asking whether a technology is possible, but what has to become repeatable, manufacturable, safe, affordable, and integrated before it can change the built world.
